# Personal Leadership

Notes from Remco Claassen on personal leadership: influence, self-control, change, energy, mission, and trust.

Source: course / talk notes. Some items (e.g. the seven golden rules for a mission statement) are named but not listed in the original notes.

---

## 1. Increase your influence

Do not blame others for your own success or failure.

Find out what you actually have influence over, and act from an internal locus of control.

Even when something large sits in your circle of concern, you still have influence over your own behaviour and choices.

Destruction can be direct. Growth, recovery, and improvement can only be influenced indirectly. That kind of process therefore needs extra attention and energy. Expand your circle of influence step by step.

---

## 2. Tame your fickle brain

Find out what gives you a "red cauliflower" — what is your allergy?

Delay your reaction. Use awareness, free will, creative imagination, and conscience.

Think about an effective response: choose behaviour that adds value (a value-adding attitude).

From your allergy, also discover your core quality, pitfall, and challenge using Ofman's core quadrant.

---

## 3. Change more intelligently

When changing things, use the leadership cross. Organisational goals and people's goals must line up, so people feel high satisfaction and contribute a lot to the organisation.

People can and want to change. They do not want to *be* changed.

Every change needs the three K's:

- **Kennis** — knowledge
- **Kunde** — skill / capability
- **Motivatie** — motivation

---

## 4. Energy scan

Reflect and discover where you get your energy from.

When what you do aligns with your talent and passion, you reach congruence. That gives you energy.

Use $E = MC^{2}$ as a balance check:

- **E** = energy
- **M** = mission (what I want)
- **C** = character (who I am)
- **C** = competence (what I can do)

Discover your values. What are your keywords, and which four matter most?

---

## 5. Everything you want in one sentence

Everything is created twice. The more carefully you think in advance about what you want, the better you can choose the actions that get you there.

A mission gives direction to your life. You base actions and goals on what matters to you, so you stand 100% behind what you do.

Follow the seven golden rules when writing your mission. *(Not listed in the source notes.)*

Typical mission format used in Claassen sessions:

> I, [first name] [last name], [sentences built from four core words].

Write it as a description of *you as a person*, not as an employee or partner.

---

## 6. From words to deeds

Work your mission statement out for each part of the "five-slice pie":

| Area | Question |
|------|----------|
| Emotional | How do I want to feel? How do I score now? Where can I grow? |
| Active | What do I want to *do*? |
| Social | How do I want to relate to others? |
| Physical | How do I want to treat my body? |
| Material | What do I want around money, stuff, and resources? |

Write living rules that help with daily choices. No end date.

Formulate one-year goals: make the end result you want explicit.

---

## 7. Winning trust

You have an emotional bank account with everyone, with deposits and withdrawals.

The more you are in credit with someone, the more that person will support you.

Credits and debits are timeless.

Watch out for mini-withdrawals.
