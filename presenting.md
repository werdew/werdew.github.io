# Presenting

Notes on stage fright, purpose, structure, persuasion, interaction, and presence.

Source: course notes. A few headings at the end were only keywords (`Aristotle`, `reference person`, `tone`). Those are expanded only where the meaning is standard, and marked where the notes themselves were incomplete.

---

## 1. Stakes and stage fright

### Stage fright

- Check whether your negative thoughts actually hold.
- Not every presentation is PowerPoint. Use tools that fit you, the message, and the audience.
- Focus on the audience, not on yourself.
- Do not set the bar too high. Mistakes make the talk human.
- The audience does not see your tension as clearly as you feel it.
- Exaggerate your disaster scenarios until they become unbelievable.
- Chat with the audience beforehand. They are just people.
- Stretch before you go on.
- Use your hands during the talk.

### What matters

A presentation lives on three things: **purpose**, **structure**, and **contact**.

## 2. Purpose, audience, and constraints

Do not start a presentation with *what*. Start with *why*.

### Purpose

Decide what the talk is for:

| Aim | Question |
|-----|----------|
| Inform | What should the audience *know*? |
| Persuade | What should the audience *think*? |
| Call to action | What should the audience *do*? |
| Entertain | What should the audience *feel / enjoy*? |

If you are presenting on assignment, the purpose must be explicit.

Write the purpose in one clear sentence.

### Audience

- What is their background and role? Business tone or personal tone? Do they know the jargon?
- How much prior knowledge do they have?
- Why are they there?
- What is their mood?

### Constraints

Invitation, location, tools, duration, and the rest of the programme.

---

## 3. Structure, language, and tools

**Structure:** introduction, body, close.

**Tools:** digital slides, flipchart, whiteboard, handouts. Pick what fits the room and the message.

---

## 4. Storytelling, persuasion, and interaction

### Persuasion

- Use only the strong arguments. Many weak ones damage credibility.
- Do not jump to defence when someone objects. Let them explain. Check that you understood. Then answer.
- Rebut the predictable counter-arguments inside the talk itself.
- Rhetorical devices: repetition, the rule of three, rhetorical question, imagery, paradox.

### Interaction

- Showing a weakness wins sympathy. You are human too.
- Take an equal stance with the audience. Do not talk down.
- If the room feels tense or negative, name the feeling out loud.
- Ask and answer questions.
- Run discussion: state a claim and let the audience react.
- Give the audience small tasks to keep them in the room.

### Handling questions

- Say when questions are welcome.
- Ask for clarification if a question is vague.
- Write the question down so everyone sees what is being answered.
- If you do not know, say you will look it up and come back later.
- Make the answer concrete with an example.
- Do not let questions drag you far off topic.
- Check that the answer was clear. Thank the person who asked before you move on.

## Staying interesting

### Drama arc

Five beats:

1. Introduce the main character. They want the far side of the arc (step 5).
2. Problems appear and keep getting larger.
3. Climax. It looks as if the goal will fail.
4. Turning point. A good decision puts the far side of the arc in sight again.
5. Resolution. It worked.

### Other keywords from the notes

| Term | Likely meaning |
|------|----------------|
| Reference person (`ijk-persoon`) | Someone the audience can identify with. In a story, often the main character. |
| Tone (`toon`) | How you sound: register, warmth, authority, humour. Match audience and purpose. |
| Aristotle | Classical persuasion: **ethos** (who you are), **pathos** (what they feel), **logos** (what follows). |
