## 2. Algorithms

**FACT** is a guideline so algorithm design stays beneficial and trustworthy:

| Letter | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| F | Fairness |
| A | Accuracy |
| C | Confidentiality |
| T | Transparency |

Public algorithms used by Dutch government bodies are listed in the [algorithm register](https://algoritmes.overheid.nl/nl).

## 3. Public space

Computers do not see photos the way we do. They analyse millions of pixels (points of light).

Deep learning finds patterns in that data by itself, layer by layer.

That is why systems can recognise almost anything: faces, number plates, luggage left on a platform.

The **AI Act** is meant to protect people against the most dangerous uses of AI in public space.

---

## 4. How smart is AI?

A language model predicts the next word. It does not understand what it writes.

Watch for:

- **Hallucinations** — confident nonsense
- **Yes-man behaviour** — agreeing too easily
- **Stale knowledge** — training data that is out of date

A single prompt uses little electricity. The real issue is the global scale of data centres.

Keep training your own brain. What you do not use, weakens.

## 5. Prompts

Give the model three things:

1. **Context** — the situation
2. **Expectation** — what it is for
3. **Desired result** — the format

---

## 6. Tips

- Use AI images mainly internally. For external work, a photographer or designer is still the safer choice.
- Publishing deepfakes or AI news externally has required a clear label since August 2026.
- A Custom GPT or Skill: set the AI up once, reuse it.
- Agents are getting more autonomous. Let them *propose*, not just *execute*, whenever the action has effects outside your screen.

## 7. Society

Data must be representative *and* used for the right purpose.

Stay alert and stay critical. That applies to people who run AI systems, and to you.

Systems should stay transparent enough that you can see how they reached a decision.

That is FACT again: fairness and transparency in practice, sometimes with large personal consequences.

Not all of your data is your property. You still have a say over it.

Automated government decisions with large effects count as **high risk**, with strict requirements for oversight and transparency.

An organisation that uses such a system is a **deployer** (`gebruiksverantwoordelijke`) — the same role your employer has if they use AI at work.

In healthcare, AI can help with diagnosis *and* paperwork, but always next to a real clinician.

Medical AI applications must meet strict requirements, including a **CE mark**.

## 8. Law

The constitution protects fundamental rights such as privacy and equal treatment. GDPR makes that concrete for data. The AI Act makes it concrete for AI systems themselves.

The AI Act uses a traffic-light model:

| Level | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| Prohibited | Not allowed |
| High risk | Strict duties |
| Limited risk | Transparency duties |
| Minimal risk | Light touch |

If you use AI at work, you (or your employer) are the deployer, with your own obligations.

**AI literacy** has been a legal duty since 2025. This course is one way to meet it. Some rules already apply; others still have a transition period.

## 9. Future

Dutch people who already work with AI judge its impact more realistically than people who do not.

In the Netherlands the main risk is not mass unemployment. It is a growing gap between people who can work with AI and people who cannot.

Agents change work from *doing it yourself* to *directing*: set goals, check, steer.

As with earlier technology shifts, some jobs go away and new ones appear.
